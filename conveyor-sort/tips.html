Tips & Strategy

The rules take a minute. Playing well takes a bit longer.

1. Bins are the real resource, not time

Beginners watch the belt. Experienced players watch the bins. The belt only hurts you when you have nowhere to put what is on it, and that is a bin problem, not a speed problem.

A bin sitting on two of three is a bin that is almost gone. A bin sitting on zero is a bin that will occupy a slot for a long time. When you have a free choice, feed the bin that is closest to sealing — clearing a slot is what buys you room for whatever arrives next.

2. Do not hoard the Joker, but do not reflex it either

The Joker fits anywhere, which makes it tempting to spend the moment it appears. It is usually worth holding for a few seconds while you see what the belt sends next.

The rule of thumb: spend the Joker on the bin that is on two of three. That converts it into a sealed bin and a free slot immediately. Spending it on an empty bin gets you a third of the way to the same result, which is a much smaller return for a piece you may not see again this level.

3. Send mystery packages to empty bins

A mystery package does not reveal its colour until it lands. If you drop it onto a bin sitting on two of three and it turns out to be the wrong colour, you have spoiled your best bin.

Dropping it into a bin that is empty or has one package costs you far less if it guesses wrong, and costs you nothing if it guesses right.

4. Bombs outrank your plan

Every other piece can wait a moment. A bomb cannot. The instant one appears, deal with it — even if that means breaking up a bin you were about to seal.

Most lost runs are not lost to the bomb itself. They are lost in the three seconds after it goes off, when the belt has kept moving and you are suddenly behind on everything else.

5. Watch the colour that has no bin

When a colour is travelling down the belt and no open bin matches it, you are on a countdown. Either a matching bin has to open before that package reaches the end, or the package is gone.

This is the situation to plan around. If you can see a colour coming with nowhere to go, prioritise sealing whichever bin frees a slot soonest, so a new bin can open in time.

6. Treat the Daily Puzzle as a separate game

The Daily Puzzle is one attempt, and the streak is the whole point. Play it when you are actually paying attention, not while you are half-watching something else. A streak takes weeks to build and one distracted morning to lose.

Because it is a fixed puzzle, it rewards a slower opening. Spend the first few packages setting up bins rather than clearing fast, and the back half becomes much easier.

7. Slow down on purpose

The belt creates a sense of urgency that is mostly psychological. Tapping fast is how you send a package to the wrong bin, and a misplaced package costs more than a second of hesitation ever will.

If you find yourself losing repeatedly at the same point, the answer is almost never "play faster."
